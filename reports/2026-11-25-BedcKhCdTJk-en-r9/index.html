The Moral Argument Playbook: How Apologists Teach It

Br. Benoy Joy, BFA apologetics class (Malayalam). One speaker, no rationalist on stage, analysed for the tactics it teaches.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BedcKhCdTJk · 2026-11-25 09:45

Br. Benoy Joy teaches Craig's moral argument: no God means no objective morality; objective morality exists; so God exists (00:17:11).

No one argues the other side. Atheists appear only as anecdotes, cartoons and edited clips, so the case wins because it is never tested.

Premise 1 rests on a serial killer, a misread author and a cut-down student clip. Premise 2 rests on gut feeling. Neither premise gets an argument.

The class mostly hands students reusable tactics, not evidence. The tactics are mapped below so they can be beaten next time.

Top 5 apologist flaws, worst first

These moves are being taught, not just used. Expect each one from students of this class.

FALLACYBr. Benoy Joy▶ 28:25–28:43
1. Smearing evolution with a serial killer
Steelman: beliefs shape behaviour, so a godless worldview removes restraint. But 'God doesn't exist, I evolved... how many did he kill? 17' (00:28:25) is an appeal to consequences built on one hand-picked case. Whether evolution is true does not depend on what Dahmer did. Hundreds of millions of people accept evolution and harm no one. His own story cuts the other way: Dahmer became a believer only after the killings (00:27:36).
STRAWMANBr. Benoy Joy▶ 45:14–47:30
2. Strawman: 'evolution means kill the old and the disabled'
He claims that if evolution grounds morality, we 'should' kill the elderly and children with autism, and that eugenics would be 'good'. This mixes up how a moral sense arose with what we ought to do. 'Evolution has a goal: survival of the fittest' (00:43:53) is also wrong, because evolution has no goals. Cooperation, kin care and care for the injured all evolved; Neanderthal skeletons show people surviving long after crippling injuries. Lions do care for cubs, including in shared nursery groups, so 'no parental care' (00:46:02) is false.
QUOTE-MINEDBr. Benoy Joy▶ 53:14–54:21
3. Quote-mining: the Cambridge student and Rifkin
The medical student says 'I agree' that murder is wrong (00:53:16). He then asks Charlie Kirk to justify why, which is exactly the grounding question. The speaker presents it as proof that atheism has left students unsure whether murder is wrong (00:54:15). He also calls Jeremy Rifkin 'a well-known evolutionist' (00:29:07), but Rifkin's book Algeny attacks Darwinism. The 'we are the kingdom, the power and the glory' line describes the worldview Rifkin opposes.
DODGEDBr. Benoy Joy▶ 1:22:35–1:24:26
4. Euthyphro: renamed, not solved
He calls the dilemma a false dichotomy and answers 'God commands because God is good' (01:22:56). That only moves the problem. Is God's nature good because it is God's, which makes goodness arbitrary? Or because it meets a standard of goodness, which makes that standard independent of God? Calling the problem a fallacy does not answer it. 'Something is good because God is good' (01:23:00) assumes the very thing in dispute.
CONTRADICTIONBr. Benoy Joy▶ 1:33:12–1:35:51
5. 'Unchanging' morality that changes with circumstances
Faced with Cain marrying his sister and the later death penalty for incest, he says God's nature stayed the same and only its 'implementation' changed with circumstances (01:35:51). That concedes the point: the rule follows harm and context, which is ordinary consequence-based ethics that needs no God. The rescue rests on false claims. There is no 'perfect DNA' in a first couple (01:34:04); population genetics puts the human ancestral population in the thousands. Children of sibling unions face serious risks, but not '100%' defects (01:35:10) or '95%' low IQ (01:35:28).

Patterns that recur across apologetics

Learn these as recognisable moves, not one-off mistakes.

UNSUPPORTEDBr. Benoy Joy▶ 57:22–57:31
Premise 2 proven by gut feeling
Objective morality is 'proven' by a gun-to-the-head anecdote, by audience agreement that rape is wrong (00:58:04), and by a child's guilty grin (01:01:52). Strong agreement shows widely shared moral intuitions. It does not show those intuitions come from outside human minds. Then 'we proved it' (01:03:38): an assertion, not a proof.
SELF-DEFEATINGBr. Benoy Joy▶ 1:00:16–1:01:30
Analogies that undercut the case
The speed limit analogy is meant to show morality is objective. But speed limits are made by people, differ by country and get changed by votes. The answer-key analogy (00:21:27) has the same problem: someone writes the key. Both analogies support the view he is arguing against.
FALSEBr. Benoy Joy▶ 34:12–35:46
Unverified history in the service of the story
'The people elected Hitler': the Nazis peaked at about 37% in a free election, and Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor. 'Thousands' of Jews killed: about six million. The Nuremberg judge's line about 'a law above all human laws' comes with no source. The tribunal actually rested on the 1945 London Charter, which is human international law.
FALSEBr. Benoy Joy▶ 47:50–50:23
Misrepresenting what atheists say, then arguing from silence
He claims the major atheists all accept that there is no morality without God (00:47:41). Sam Harris wrote a whole book, The Moral Landscape, arguing the opposite. He then treats an unanswered livestream question as proof that no answer exists (00:50:23), which is an argument from silence. His scenario of caging women to raise birth rates also defines 'flourishing' as head-count. On a well-being view, mass suffering rules it out immediately.
AD POPULUMBr. Benoy Joy▶ 55:37–56:32
Popularity and law as validation
Rising Bible sales and a Texas Ten Commandments law are offered as signs of truth. Sales and statutes measure demand and politics, not whether God exists.
PATTERNBr. Benoy Joy▶ 1:26:27–1:26:32
The real goal: confidence, not inquiry
'We have good answers' is taught as a starting assumption, apologetics is framed as a tool for evangelism (00:11:07), and Romans 2:15 is read as evidence for conscience (01:02:17). Scripture here is the claim being tested, not evidence for it. The method starts from the conclusion and works backwards.

Atheist voices in the room: how they should have been answered

No rationalist spoke. These are the atheist positions as reported, with how each should be pressed.

STRONG POINTBr. Benoy Joy▶ 1:27:03–1:27:55
The classmate's incest question was the strongest point of the night
The speaker admits he had no answer at the time. Correction: press the follow-up. 'If God changes the rule because of harm, then harm is doing the moral work. Why do you still need God?'
HOW TO ANSWERBr. Benoy Joy▶ 31:58–32:49
'According to whom?'
Turn it around: 'according to God' needs its own 'which God, and who says so?' He names Osama bin Laden as the man who thought himself good (00:32:03). Bin Laden was a theist. Belief in God did not settle his moral confusion.
HOW TO ANSWERBr. Benoy Joy▶ 59:44–59:52
Michael Ruse's 2+2=5 line
Correction: we know arithmetic is objective without grounding it in God. Moral facts about suffering and well-being can be just as objective without a lawgiver.
MISSED OPENINGBr. Benoy Joy▶ 58:13–58:20
'Was rape right in BC?'
Correction: point to the Bible's own law. Deuteronomy 22:28-29 is widely read as making a rapist pay the father and marry the victim, and Leviticus 25:44-46 allows slavery. If God's morality never changes, those laws are still good. If they are not, it changed.
HOW TO ANSWERBr. Benoy Joy▶ 39:42–40:24
'Morality is just social convention'
Do not accept his framing that morality is either subjective whim or divine command. Moral realism without God, contractualism and well-being ethics all condemn sati and child marriage on stated reasons. The Iraqi marriage-law change he cites (00:33:50) came from religious-sect law, not from secular majority opinion.
CONCLUSIONThe class never proves either premise. Premise 1 rests on a killer's anecdote and two misread sources; premise 2 rests on intuitions the room already shared. The repeated method is to attack a cartoon of evolution, appeal to horror, redefine dilemmas instead of answering them, and call the result proof. The reply that beats it: shared moral intuitions are evidence of shared human nature, not of a lawgiver. And a God whose rules change when harm changes has already admitted that harm is doing the moral work.
