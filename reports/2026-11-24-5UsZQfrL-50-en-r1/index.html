Does the Biblical God exist? Anup Isaac vs Asher John

A Malayalam debate, about 189 minutes. A fact-check and analysis for reading on a phone.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5UsZQfrL-50 · 2026-11-25 05:08

Anup Isaac (atheist) argued that the God described in the Bible contradicts itself: its listed attributes clash with each other, with science, and with the God's own recorded actions.

Asher John (Christian) argued that this God is the best explanation for three things: the Big Bang, the survival and success of the Jewish people, and the resurrection of Jesus.

Anup's core case came out stronger on substance. His moral contradictions, the failed 'this generation' prophecy and the problem of evil were mostly waved off as 'off topic' rather than answered.

Asher was more polished and more often right on context. But his case leaned on quotes from authorities and on 'if God, then X; X happened; so God' reasoning. Several of his facts were wrong.

Both men made factual errors. Anup was wrong about polio, Onan, and what death 'by definition' rules out.

A note on who said what

The automatic speaker labels in the record are often mixed up. 'Speaker 1' often carries Anup's lines, and the 'Anup Isaac' label sometimes carries Asher's. Below, each point is credited by what was said, not by the label. The timestamps are copied from the record.

Anup's strongest points

These stood up to scrutiny and were not properly answered.

STRONG POINTAnup Isaac▶ 7:15–7:40
Matching the Bible's timeline to the universe's age fails
The Bible's genealogies add up to thousands of years since creation. The universe is about 13.8 billion years old (Planck satellite data). Anup was careful: he did not claim an exact Biblical figure. Asher never answered this directly.
STRONG POINTAnup Isaac▶ 8:08–8:58
Genesis puts the Earth before the Sun, and light before the Sun
In Genesis 1 there is light on day one and the Sun on day four, after the Earth. Astronomy says the Sun formed before or alongside the Earth, about 4.6 billion years ago. Asher's reply (Herodotus also made errors) fits a fallible human text. It does not fit a text authored by an all-knowing God.
STRONG POINTAnup Isaac▶ 2:08:22–2:08:38
'This generation shall not pass away'
Matthew 24:34 says the end-times events would come within the listeners' generation. Many mainstream New Testament scholars treat this as an end-of-the-world prediction that did not come true. Asher offered fulfilled prophecies instead, and never addressed this one.
STRONG POINTAnup Isaac▶ 50:42–52:30
The problem of evil, made concrete
His example was the rape and murder of a young woman on a Kerala train by Govindachamy. An all-powerful, all-knowing, loving God could have prevented it at trivial cost. Mark 16:16 then sends the unbaptised victim to judgment. Asher gave no theodicy, meaning no account of why such a God allows this. He only offered a general 'the Fall' line later (02:34:05).
ACCURATEAnup Isaac▶ 2:15:00–2:15:15
The texts he quoted say what he said they say
He quoted: Exodus 21:20-21 (a slave beaten to death is punished only if they die at once, 'for he is his money'), Numbers 31:17-18 (keep the virgin girls), 2 Kings 2:23-24 (bears maul 42 boys), Leviticus 20:13 and Deuteronomy 13. All are quoted fairly. Calling such a God 'just' and 'merciful' is a real tension that the Bible's defenders have to resolve.
STRONG POINTAnup Isaac▶ 2:54:36–2:55:20
Jesus said the Law stands, against Asher's 'the Law was imperfect'
Asher said God gave Moses an imperfect law (02:45:22). Anup cited Matthew 5:17-18: not one jot or tittle will pass from the Law. That is a fair tension inside the text. Christian theology has answers (fulfilment rather than abolition), but Asher's claim of an 'imperfect law from a perfect God' needs an argument he never gave.
FAIR POINTAnup Isaac▶ 2:51:31–2:51:49
Who made the creator?
If everything needs a cause, so does God. If God is exempt, the exemption needs a reason. Without one it is special pleading (exempting your own case from the rule). Classical theists reply that only things that begin need a cause. But Asher never made that argument, so the point stood.
FAIR POINTAnup Isaac▶ 2:09:28–2:09:57
A plausible origin for belief in gods
Over-detecting agents (rustling leaf = predator) helped our ancestors survive. This matches the cognitive science of religion's 'hyperactive agency detection'. It explains why belief is common. It does not prove that God is absent, and Anup did not claim it did.

Asher's main arguments and where they fail

Each is put in its strongest form first.

FALLACYAsher John▶ 29:13–29:18
The Big Bang 'confirms' Genesis
Strongest form: Genesis said the universe had a beginning, many ancient thinkers said it was eternal, and cosmology now points to a beginning. The problem: the Big Bang describes expansion from a hot, dense state. Whether that was an absolute beginning is still open. Being compatible with Genesis is not confirmation. Many creation stories have beginnings, and Genesis 1:2 itself starts with pre-existing waters.
FALLACYAsher John▶ 30:22–30:41
Jewish survival and success prove God
The structure is 'If God, then Jewish survival; survival happened; so God.' That is affirming the consequent: the conclusion does not follow. Other persecuted minorities also survived, such as Armenians, Parsis and Samaritans. Scholars do offer natural explanations, for example Botticini and Eckstein's work on literacy and urban trades. Calling it something that 'defies natural explanation' (00:36:29) is an argument from ignorance.
UNFALSIFIABLEAsher John▶ 32:22–32:32
The prophecy is too vague to fail
'Blessed, scattered, persecuted, surviving' fits almost any outcome. Success counts as blessing, persecution counts as prophecy, and survival counts as protection. If no outcome could have counted against it, it is not evidence. Anup's reply at 01:46:13 also lands: a God who favours one people is hard to square with 'just to all'.
OVERSTATEDAsher John▶ 39:47–40:38
The resurrection 'minimal facts'
Strongest form: crucifixion, followers sincerely reporting appearances, and Paul's and James's conversions are widely accepted. Asher is right on the crucifixion and the appearance experiences. But the empty tomb is not 'almost universally accepted'. Even Gary Habermas, who popularised this approach, puts it at about 75% of scholars. Sincere belief and martyrdom show that people believed, not that the belief was true.
QUOTE-MININGAsher John▶ 1:23:46–1:24:21
Quoting sceptics who reject his conclusion
Lüdemann did write that the disciples' appearance experiences are historically certain. But he explained them as visions and denied the resurrection. Antony Flew (01:19:12) likewise stayed unconvinced of the resurrection. Asher partly admitted this at 01:19:30, but used both men as support anyway.
AUTHORITYAsher John▶ 1:18:32–1:18:37
The rest of his case is mostly argument from authority
He quoted Penzias, Bauer, Isham, Durant, Mark Twain, Wright and others, and demanded that Anup counter with citations. Experts summarising data can be evidence. 'X said it is compatible with the Bible' is not. Asher's own Stanford Encyclopedia definition (01:36:20) is fair: Anup was wrong to dismiss all expert quotes as a fallacy.
CIRCULARAsher John▶ 2:02:25–2:04:41
Hematidrosis proves Jesus had foreknowledge
Hematidrosis (sweating blood) is real, and the 2009 Indian Journal of Dermatology article he cited exists. But Luke 22:44 (not chapter 12) says sweat 'like drops of blood'. The verse is missing from several of the earliest manuscripts. Using the story to prove Jesus' foreknowledge assumes the story is accurate in the first place.
OVERSTATEDAsher John▶ 2:05:00–2:05:50
Isaiah 53 predicted the crucifixion
The Great Isaiah Scroll does date to about 125 BCE. But Isaiah 53 never mentions crucifixion, and Jewish readers have long taken the servant to be Israel. The Gospel writers knew Isaiah and may have told the story with it in mind. That makes the match weak evidence.

Asher's factual errors

FALSEAsher John▶ 27:15–27:28
Lemaître and 'let there be light'
Asher said Lemaître credited Genesis with leading him to the Big Bang. The record shows the opposite. Lemaître kept science and faith separate, and objected when Pope Pius XII linked the Big Bang to creation in 1951.
FALSEAsher John▶ 28:16
Where Penzias worked
Penzias and Wilson found the cosmic background radiation at Bell Labs, not a university in Iowa. The 1978 Nobel and the New York Times quote are real.
FALSEAsher John▶ 2:19:55–2:20:13
Atheist physicists oppose the Big Bang
Big Bang cosmology is the consensus across religious and non-religious physicists. Hoyle's steady-state model lost decades ago. Debate today is about what came before or caused it, not whether it happened.
MISATTRIBUTEDAsher John▶ 30:00–30:15
Pascal and Louis XIV
The 'the Jews, Your Majesty' story is usually told of Frederick the Great and his physician. It is a story, not evidence.
UNPROVENAsher John▶ 37:53–39:01
Sennacherib's army destroyed by a miracle
Sennacherib's own prism says he trapped Hezekiah 'like a bird in a cage' and took tribute. Herodotus' story is about mice and an Egyptian campaign. Historians agree only that Jerusalem was not taken. Moving from 'unexplained' to 'angel' is god of the gaps (using God to fill a gap in knowledge).
FALSEAsher John▶ 2:28:36–2:28:44
Tiberius has no source for 100 years
Velleius Paterculus wrote during Tiberius' reign, and Philo and Valerius Maximus wrote close to it. Asher is right that Jesus' existence is the scholarly consensus. This comparison is wrong.
FALSEAsher John▶ 1:37:12–1:37:17
Richard Carrier is 'just a blogger'
Carrier holds a PhD in ancient history from Columbia. His view that Jesus never existed is a fringe position, which is a fair point. Calling him unqualified is not.
FALSEAsher John▶ 1:24:36–1:24:47
Atheists refuse to debate the resurrection
Ehrman, Lüdemann, Carrier and others have publicly debated William Lane Craig, Licona and others on exactly this. Even if a local group declined, that is an argument from silence, not proof.
HALF-TRUEAsher John▶ 2:42:29–2:43:20
Galileo was punished for theology, not science
Numbers' book 'Galileo Goes to Jail' (Harvard University Press) is real, and it rejects the simple 'church vs science' myth. Bruno was condemned mainly for theological heresy. But Galileo was condemned in 1633 specifically for teaching heliocentrism (that the Earth goes round the Sun). Copernicus was never persecuted. 'Unanimous agreement' is overstated.
OVERSTATEDAsher John▶ 2:01:29–2:01:49
The Temple prophecy
Most scholars date Mark to about 66-70 CE, not 40 CE. That leaves open that the prophecy was written after the event. The claim that the 'UN tried' to rebuild the Temple has no basis.

Anup's factual errors and weak arguments

Fairness cuts both ways. These hurt his case.

FALSEAnup Isaac▶ 47:53–48:06
Polio is not congenital
Polio is a viral infection caught after birth, not a defect present at birth. Salk's vaccine dates from 1955, not '200 or 300 years' ago (03:03:08). His wider point, that humans lacked protection for millennia, survives. The facts he used for it do not.
MISREADINGAnup Isaac▶ 15:04–15:27
Onan misread
Genesis 38:9 says Onan knew the child would legally count as his dead brother's (levirate duty, the custom of fathering an heir for a dead brother). He was not confused about biology. The killing for refusing still raises a moral question. The 'God should have taught him biology' joke does not.
EQUIVOCATIONAnup Isaac▶ 21:00–21:39
Deuteronomy 32:4 does not say God 'doesn't know evil'
The verse says God is 'without iniquity': he does no wrong. Swapping 'does no evil' for 'knows no evil' creates the clash with God being everywhere. This contradiction is manufactured.
BEGS QUESTIONAnup Isaac▶ 46:54–47:33
'Resurrection is impossible by definition'
Defining death as 'final' assumes what is in dispute. The New Testament also says the Father raised Jesus (Acts 2:24), and the Trinity distinguishes Father and Son. 'I and the Father are one' (John 10:30, cited 03:04:21) is not read as 'same person'. The better atheist point is that the claim is unproven, not that it is self-contradictory.
CONTESTEDAnup Isaac▶ 16:29–17:26
Knowing everything vs being all-powerful, and vs free will
He repeated it at 02:53:00. It is a real philosophical puzzle, but presented as a proof it commits a modal fallacy. Knowing what will happen does not force it to happen. Philosophers on both sides dispute it. It is not a settled contradiction.
WEAKAnup Isaac▶ 9:41–10:25
Pi = 3 and falling stars
1 Kings 7:23 gives rough measurements of a cast basin. Brim thickness or rounding can explain it. The falling stars in Matthew 24:29 are apocalyptic imagery. These are the weakest of his 'scientific errors' and distract from the strong ones (age, order of creation).
OVERSTATEDAnup Isaac▶ 2:51:00–2:51:25
'A universe from nothing' overclaimed
A zero-total-energy universe is a serious hypothesis, not established science. Particle pairs arise in a quantum vacuum, which has physical properties; that is not 'nothing'. Hawking radiation has not been observed. The honest answer to 'who made the universe' is 'we don't know', and that leaves the burden of proof with the theist.
OVERSTATEDAnup Isaac▶ 1:54:33–1:54:54
Hosea 13:16 framed as a command
The verse foretells what invaders will do to Samaria as divine punishment. That is still morally troubling. But it is not an order to Israel to kill pregnant women.
OVERREACHAnup Isaac▶ 1:31:41–1:31:54
'No Christian can have humanity'
He said he targets the religion, not people (01:13:44). This line breaks that rule. Calling the Eucharist cannibalism also ignores that most Christians see it as symbolic or sacramental.

Where Asher was right

He scored real points that Anup did not answer.

STRONG POINTAsher John▶ 55:04–55:35
Errors in a book don't erase its subject
Herodotus and Plutarch contain errors, yet Sennacherib and Caesar existed. This defeats 'the Bible has errors, so no God exists' against God in general. It does not defeat Anup's narrower target: a God who is the inerrant author of the text and has these exact attributes.
ACCURATEAsher John▶ 1:38:08–1:38:23
Women followers are in the text
Luke 8:1-3 names women who travelled with and funded Jesus. Anup's claim was correct only for the Twelve.
FAIR POINTAsher John▶ 2:17:41–2:18:29
Matthew 10:34, 'not peace but a sword'
Read in context, the passage is about families dividing over faith. It is not a call to violence. Mainstream scholarship supports Asher here.
ACCURATEAsher John▶ 2:27:36–2:28:10
Jesus existed
Nearly all academic historians accept a historical Jesus. Anup did not deny it (01:06:45). Asher was arguing against Kerala rationalists who were not present.
PARTLY RIGHTAsher John▶ 1:58:12–1:58:36
The Bible condemns child sacrifice elsewhere
Leviticus 18:21 and Deuteronomy 12:31 do ban it. But Anup is right that Judges 11:29 puts the Spirit on Jephthah just before his vow, and the text never condemns the sacrifice. The narrative is ambiguous, not clearly disapproving.

Dodges and rhetorical moves

DODGEDAsher John▶ 1:15:11–1:15:25
'Off topic' used to avoid the moral contradictions
The debate was titled as a Biblical one. If the God's defining attributes conflict with the God's recorded deeds, that is on topic. Asher repeatedly said Anup offered 'no argument' instead of answering the flood, Onan, the famine cannibalism (2 Kings 6) or Govindachamy.
BURDEN SHIFTAsher John▶ 54:57
Shifting the burden of proof
He demanded that Anup 'prove God does not exist'. The person claiming God exists carries the burden. Anup took on extra burden voluntarily with his method of contradiction, and he met it for the inerrant, all-good God he defined.
AD HOMINEMAsher John▶ 1:56:02–1:56:07
Insults instead of answers
He called Kerala atheism 'intellectually bankrupt'. He also called it a cult (02:42:15) and told an audience member they had 'ignorance plus arrogance' (02:39:43). Name-calling rebuts no argument.
OVERREACHAnup Isaac▶ 1:27:11–1:27:16
Dismissing all expert evidence
Calling every scholarly citation an 'appeal to authority' goes too far. Relevant expert consensus is evidence. The fair objection is that Asher's quotes were often misused, not that quoting experts is illegitimate.
DODGEDAsher John▶ 2:32:38
Ducking the question about Islam
He refused to say whether Muslims' lower Nobel share tells against Allah. That same logic was the core of his Jewish-success argument, so applying it to Islam would expose the reasoning. He declined to.
CONCLUSIONAnup's central claim holds up on the record. The God described in the Bible, all-good, all-just and authoring an error-free book, clashes with its own text, with cosmology, and with prophecy that did not come true. Asher mostly avoided these points instead of answering them. Anup weakened his case with avoidable errors: polio, Onan, Deuteronomy 32:4, and 'impossible by definition'. He also overclaimed on physics. The honest atheist line is 'unproven', not 'impossible'. Asher's positive case did not meet the burden of proof. Compatibility with the Big Bang is not confirmation. Jewish survival is a vague prophecy argued in a circle. The resurrection 'facts' quote sceptics who reject his conclusion. He was right on context several times, and his speeches were clear and well organised. But more examples make 'if God, then X; X; so God' no more logically valid.
